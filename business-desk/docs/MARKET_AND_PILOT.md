# Business Desk: market, workflow and paid-pilot plan

Research date: **4 October 2026**. This combines public primary sources, the owner's stated needs, and product hypotheses. No customer interviews, usage recordings, willingness-to-pay survey or market-size estimate has been invented.

## Recommended position

Sell a configured daily operating workspace with local onboarding and support: **know what to do next, collect money on time, and keep work and stock connected**. A long list of modules will not differentiate the product on its own.

Use one business core with four profiles: garage/collision, showroom, retail/trading, and service/repair. Start with 3–5 observed businesses across these profiles. Give each a workspace suited to its vocabulary, roles, work stages and forms. Avoid maintaining a separate code fork for every customer: that makes bug fixes, tax-format changes and support expensive.

The first commercial offer should be an assisted pilot with an explicit scope. Do not promise to replace all accounting, banking, dealer systems, payroll compliance and customer communications immediately.

## Pain points to validate, and the corresponding daily behaviour

| Business problem | Staff's actual job | Product response | What to measure in the pilot |
|---|---|---|---|
| Owner has to ask everyone for updates | Find delayed jobs, unanswered enquiries and money promised today | One daily action list generated from record conditions, with a dated next action | Number of missed promised dates; time spent preparing the morning review |
| Customer credit is spread across notebooks/messages | Find balance, record part-payment, agree the next contact | Retained receipts, advances, allocations and a collection queue | Incorrect balances, overdue follow-ups, minutes to answer a balance question |
| Same customer details are typed repeatedly | Move an enquiry through quote/work/bill | Shared customer records and linked documents; quick customer entry within billing | Repeated fields typed; first-bill time; duplicate customer rate |
| Actual stock differs from the system | Receive goods, consume parts, sell, return or correct stock | Retained movements; partial receipts; issue-on-invoice; no double deduction for job parts | Variance on sampled high-value/fast-moving items; unrecorded issues |
| Staff avoid a complex application | Complete one short task from a phone | Sector-specific navigation, role-relevant access, short primary actions | Unassisted completion rate; help requests per task; number of abandoned forms |
| Network failure creates distrust | Keep a draft, know whether money was recorded | Explicit pending queue, encrypted drafts, retry IDs and conflict review | Lost/duplicate entries after controlled disconnect tests; unresolved queue age |
| Sales commissions cause arguments | Agree the basis, see eligibility, avoid double payment | Recorded basis/percentage/fixed amount, collection eligibility, payroll reservation | Number of disputed earnings; independent reconciliation accuracy |
| Software support becomes a hidden burden | Get a usable answer and fix a data problem | Scoped onboarding, operator runbook, clear exports and audit trail | Support minutes per business per month; incidents requiring direct DB access |

These are hypotheses grounded in the requested workflows and product categories. Observation must establish which are frequent and valuable enough to pay for.

## Sector workflows and what “custom” should mean

### Garage / dent, paint and collision

The service adviser records the customer, vehicle, complaint and promised date. An estimate uses reusable service/part lines; approval records the precise revision. The job then carries technician assignment, blockers and a checklist. Parts and task hours connect to that work. The owner checks direct contribution, bills the agreed scope and follows the unpaid balance.

Insurance work has a separate payer/approval timeline. Capture insurer/surveyor references, original supporting documents, customer share, insurer share and settlement follow-up. Supplementary repairs need an explicit approved revision or additional work record. Keep hypothetical comparison scenarios clearly labelled; do not generate a document that purports to be an independent competitor's quote.

Pilot questions: Who authorises extra work? Which jobs stop for parts? How many vehicles can occupy each bay? Who may hand over a vehicle with an unpaid balance? Does the insurer pay the garage or reimburse the customer? Which photos and signatures are actually required?

### Small vehicle showroom

An enquiry needs a source, customer contact, salesperson, desired variant, next contact and finance status. Vehicle inventory is one record per chassis, with condition, arrival date, price and location. Reservation must be exclusive. Booking money is a customer advance until an invoice exists; allocate it later rather than recording cash twice. Invoicing allocates the vehicle. Delivery checks collection plus PDI, registration/document readiness and signed handover.

Track lost reasons and aged available stock. A salesperson's promised follow-up matters more than a generic task list with hundreds of entries. Finance approval is not receipt of money; the app must reflect collection separately.

Pilot questions: New or used vehicles? Trade-ins? Multiple yards? When is title transferred? Who funds a booking refund? Are commission agreements based on collected revenue, margin or fixed delivery incentives? Do vehicles arrive through consignment? Which manufacturer systems must remain authoritative?

Trade-ins, consignment accounting, lender integration, statutory used-vehicle tax treatment and resale after an issued/credited sale are not claimed as implemented in this candidate.

### Retail / trading

The cashier needs a fast catalogue, quantity/rate entry, customer choice and receipt. Issuing the bill consumes stock once. Credit customers require a clear due date and searchable balance. Purchases should separate ordered, physically received, supplier-billed and supplier-paid amounts.

Pilot questions: How many bills at the busiest hour? Barcode scanner and thermal/A4 printer models? Wholesale price lists? Credit limits? Unit conversions? Returns/exchanges? Multiple stock locations? Are opening stock and unpaid balances trustworthy enough to import?

Barcode scanning, thermal layouts, customer-specific price lists, credit limits, serial/batch expiry inventory, general supplier returns and full warehouse transfers need separate acceptance before they become a sales promise.

### Service and repair teams

Start with a request, appointment and asset; assign work with a clear due date. A repeat service agreement needs a renewal action and actual scheduled visits. Staff should record progress and blockers without seeing payroll or irrelevant financial settings.

Pilot questions: At-site or workshop work? Travel time and charges? Recurring contracts? Proof of completion? Repeat-service interval? Customer approvals before parts? What happens when a technician works without coverage?

Geolocation, route optimisation, customer self-service booking and automatic recurring billing are future modules, not a requirement to sell the first useful service workspace.

## Configuration boundaries

| Configure per business | Keep standard for everyone | Build as a separately scoped integration |
|---|---|---|
| Module visibility, work-stage names, catalogue, payment terms, follow-up intervals, print terms, roles, business focus | Monetary calculation, permissions, audit, immutable posting, conflict handling, backups and numbering safeguards | IRD/CBMS, bank/wallet confirmation, SMS/WhatsApp delivery, dealer/manufacturer systems, biometrics |

A support person should configure a business in one session without editing source code. The app currently includes the profiles, module selection and operational settings. Deeper custom fields and declarative workflow rules should wait until several pilots need the same pattern.

## Monetisation hypotheses

Start by charging for a maintained, configured service with support. Public source availability does not prevent charging for installation, hosting, backups, upgrades, migration and agreed support. It does mean that “nobody can copy the code” is not the business model.

The following are **test prices**, not validated market demand or a profit forecast:

| Offer to test | Illustrative price | Included scope | Explicit boundary |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Local assisted setup | NPR 5,000–12,000 once, then an optional maintenance agreement | Installation, one profile, agreed catalogue/customer import, one training session | No managed online uptime or offsite backup commitment unless separately supplied |
| Connected business pilot | NPR 1,500–2,500/month, plus scoped onboarding | One organisation/branch, agreed staff count, private hosting, updates, backup monitoring and bounded support | No bespoke code, statutory payroll engine or unimplemented integrations |
| Garage/showroom operations | NPR 2,500–4,000/month after value is proven | Sector setup, linked enquiry/job/vehicle workflows, commission configuration, agreed training/support | Multi-branch, manufacturer connections and special accounting priced only after discovery |

Do not use aggressive user limits that make staff share one login. Limits should reflect support and infrastructure costs while preserving accountability. Do not withhold access to a customer's records or export as a pressure tactic during a billing dispute. A read-only/export grace policy should be part of the commercial terms if subscription enforcement is added later.

For each customer, calculate contribution as subscription revenue minus allocated hosting, backup storage, payment fees and support time. For example, **assumptions only**: NPR 2,000 revenue − NPR 500 allocated infrastructure − NPR 300 for 30 minutes of support at NPR 600/hour = NPR 1,200 before acquisition, development, overhead and taxes. Two hours of monthly support would reduce this to NPR 300 on the same assumptions. Measure support time; it can dominate server cost.

The candidate does not contain subscription checkout, license enforcement or automatic renewal collection. The first paid pilots can use an agreed service invoice and manual payment confirmation. Add payment-provider integration only after a provider/account and commercial model are selected.

## Four-week pilot with 3–5 businesses

Recruit, if possible: one collision garage, one general service garage, one small showroom, one retailer and one service/repair team. This is workflow coverage, not a statistically representative sample. Obtain permission to observe the work and avoid copying unrelated customer data into research notes.

**Before onboarding:** spend 60–90 minutes observing the owner and the person doing billing. Ask them to show the last delayed job, unpaid bill, stock correction and commission dispute. Record the steps and time; do not start by asking which dashboard colours they like. Define the accounting/tax boundaries with their accountant before any live invoice.

**Week 1 — first useful day:** set up one profile, actual work stages, staff permissions and a small verified catalogue. Import only reviewed opening data. Complete the acceptance script with fictional transactions, then a limited real workflow after the billing/recovery gates pass. Measure time to first correct bill and the number of times staff need help.

**Week 2 — daily use:** observe the busiest hour. Review overdue actions with the owner for ten minutes each day. Capture abandoned forms, repeat typing and printer issues. Fix workflow blockers before adding another module.

**Week 3 — exceptions:** test partial delivery, partial payment, customer advance allocation, revised quote, stock correction, staff absence, commission eligibility and a controlled internet outage. Reconcile a sample of actual invoices, receipts and stock movements independently. Perform a restore drill.

**Week 4 — paid decision:** ask the owner to choose a concrete priced offer. Record acceptance, refusal and reasons. Compare observed work with the baseline. Agree a support scope and success criteria for the next month. A verbal compliment is not a paid conversion.

Suggested targets to validate: first ordinary bill within five minutes after initial setup; common repeat-customer bill within 60–90 seconds; at least 90% of sampled primary tasks completed without help; no unexplained money/stock discrepancy; all sampled overdue promises visible; zero lost or duplicate financial entries in interruption tests. These are proposed acceptance thresholds, not achieved results.

## Product sequence after this candidate

1. Fix observed billing, offline, printing and recovery problems from pilots. Add a safe supplier correction/credit workflow and showroom returns before customers who need them go live.
2. Add practical Nepali language/terminology, verified BS date/fiscal handling, faster catalogue search, imports with preview and duplicate resolution, and tested receipt printers where demand appears repeatedly.
3. Add governed customer communications: templates, consent/preferences, preview, approval, delivery status and cost controls. Start with one selected provider/channel.
4. Add credit limits, price lists, stock transfers and return workflows when the retail pilots establish rules and expected exceptions.
5. Add commercial provisioning/subscriptions and stronger tenant architecture only after the private-instance pilot economics and support model work.
6. Consider a richer AI assistant only when real structured records, access rules and user-approved actions exist. It should cite the underlying record and ask before sending messages or changing financial documents. A generic chatbot is not a substitute for complete workflows.

## Questions for the next owner interview

- Which three tasks caused the most lost time or money last week? Show the latest example of each.
- Who enters information, who approves it, and who needs only a phone view?
- What must keep working during an outage: draft capture, issuing official bills, taking payments, or all three? Which documents can wait for server confirmation?
- How many daily transactions, concurrent users and locations are there at peak?
- What are the exact forms and printouts customers, insurers, suppliers and the accountant already accept?
- How are deposits, refunds, customer credit, supplier credit, commissions and salary advances handled now?
- Which records can be imported confidently, and who will validate opening balances and stock?
- What measurable result would make the owner pay the proposed monthly price after 30 days?

Use the answers to configure and test each pilot. Do not convert a single unusual request into a permanent promise for every sector.
